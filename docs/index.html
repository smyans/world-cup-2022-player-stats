What wins World Cup matches?

Every pass, shot, tackle and press from all 64 matches of the 2022 FIFA World Cup, used to answer one question: which numbers actually go with winning, and does it depend on where you play?

Which player stats go with winning?

Spearman correlation between each per-90 stat and the match result (loss → draw → win), for 1,643 player performances of 30+ minutes. Numbers appear only where the link survives a false-discovery-rate correction. Click any cell to compare winners and losers.

View as table
Attackers: get in the box. xG, shots, touches in the box, progressive carries and xA are the only stats that clear the bar. The median attacker on a winning team racks up nearly three times the xG of one on a losing team.
Midfield and defence: no single stat wins. Nothing survives correction. Team success there is spread across the unit, not visible in one player's box score.
Pass completion % is a non-factor for every position (|ρ| < 0.08). Tidy passing isn't the same as hurting the opponent.

Where winners have the ball

Each zone shows the share of a team's on-ball actions (passes, carries, dribbles, shots, receptions) that happen there, averaged over the 49 decisive matches. Teams always attack left to right.

Can the stats call the winner?

A logistic regression compares the two teams in each decisive match on eight in-game stats (team minus opponent). It is scored with leave-one-match-out cross-validation, so every prediction below is for a match the model never saw.

What the model leans on

Effect on the log-odds of winning for one standard deviation more than the opponent, with 95% bootstrap intervals.

Every decisive match, as the model saw it

Each dot is a match, placed at the model's probability that the actual winner would win. Dots left of 50% are matches it got wrong.

Biggest surprises

Wins the stats said shouldn't have happened.

More of the ball, fewer wins. Possession is the one stat with a clearly negative weight. Partly that's game state: teams that fall behind chase the game and see more of the ball. Partly it's the tournament itself (Japan beat both Spain and Germany with under 27% of the passes).
Defending counts. Out-tackling, out-intercepting and out-blocking the opponent carries about as much weight as xG.
The flip side: the model can't explain wins built on possession. England 6–2 Iran reads as an "upset" because England had so much of the ball.

Method & caveats

Data

StatsBomb Open Data for the 2022 World Cup: 234,637 events and full lineups for all 64 matches.

Player stats

26 metrics per player per match, converted to per-90 rates using minutes reconstructed from lineups (stoppage and extra time included). Players under 30 minutes are dropped. Position is the one played longest. Penalty-shootout kicks are excluded everywhere.

Statistics

Spearman correlation with result (loss 0, draw 0.5, win 1), with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across 26 tests per position group. The win model is a symmetric logistic regression (no intercept) on standardised stat differences, scored with leave-one-match-out cross-validation.

Caveats

Correlation isn't causation, and in-game stats are partly caused by the score. Players in the same match share an outcome. It's one tournament: 64 matches, 49 with a winner.
